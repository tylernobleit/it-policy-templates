# it-policy-templates

Plain-language IT policy and planning templates for small businesses (roughly 5 to 50 staff) that do not have a dedicated IT department. Each is short enough to actually be read, with bracketed placeholders to fill in.

## Templates

| Template | Purpose |
|---|---|
| [Acceptable Use Policy](templates/acceptable-use-policy.md) | What staff may and may not do with company devices, accounts and data |
| [Incident Response Plan](templates/incident-response-plan.md) | Who does what when something goes wrong, from detection through lessons learned |
| [Backup and Disaster Recovery Plan](templates/backup-and-disaster-recovery-plan.md) | What is backed up, how often, how it is restored, and recovery targets |
| [Employee Onboarding/Offboarding Checklist](templates/onboarding-offboarding-checklist.md) | Account, device and access steps when people join or leave |

## How to use them

1. Copy a template and replace every `[BRACKETED]` placeholder
2. Delete anything that does not apply and add what is specific to the business
3. Have the business owner (or legal counsel for regulated industries) review and approve it
4. Have staff read and acknowledge the policy, and set a review date (at least yearly)

## Notes

- These are starting points, not legal advice. Businesses in regulated industries (healthcare, finance, education, government contracting) have additional requirements, such as HIPAA, PCI DSS or contractual obligations, that need qualified review.
- They are aligned in spirit with common frameworks (NIST CSF, CIS Controls) but do not claim compliance with any of them.
- Keep policies short, enforceable, and tied to actual practice. A policy nobody follows is worse than none.
